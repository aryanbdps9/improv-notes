Adjective Name Karam

We picked a word that started with the first letter of your first name and a motion to go with it. Then we passed our names and motions around the circle as a way of learning names. We will be doing this for many weeks in class, please remember your name and motion for next week!

Like Me

Someone says something that is true for them, and then we stand in proximity to them with how true that is for us

Clap Circle

This will be part of the ritual of our class, we will do this every week. We pass a clap around a circle, by simply making eye contact and clapping, aiming to go as quickly as we can. We strive to turn off, or at least notice, when the internal editor in our brains tries to make it more rigid or challenging than just pass energy around a circle. Due to the size of our class, we did this in multiple groups.

Word Ball

Also a part of the ritual of our class. Instead of clapping, we now pass words around, aiming to free associate off of the last word we just heard. There is no right or wrong word, there is just the word you say. Including if it's more than one word, a sound, or even "blank". We don't judge ourselves and we don't judge others. We also dare to be dull, our offers don't need to be clever.

We defined a few terms and practiced what they felt like.

We are striving to say yes, instead of block, and make bold statements instead of waffling.

🚫 Blocking

Denying Reality. This is when we say no to the offer of the reality of the scene that has been established. Example:

Person A:Hi Grandma!
Person B:I'm not your Grandma, I'm your father!

This makes us work way harder than we need to tell stories. It also feels bad, like the offer the person made wasn't good enough. It also makes our stories less collaborative and stay in the same place. There is safety in saying no, it means we know we are now going to have an argument about the basics of the scene. But our goal is to free fall into the unknown, and be rewarded with adventures.

🧇 Waffling

Refusing to define reality. This is when we ask questions about what's happening in the scene as opposed to making statements defining what's going on.

Example:

Person A:Hi Grandma!
Person B:Hi! What are you doing?

We want to be creating the worlds and stories that we are in together. Asking questions about the details can be a way of 1) stalling, but 2) putting all the work of defining things on our scene partners. None of us actually know the answers to the questions, we are all making it up! So we can instead make statements to our scene partners that generously provide details, so that our story can move forward and that the work isn't on one person

✅ Yes And

Accept the reality and add to it! This is what we are striving to do (though we all make mistakes...more on that over the next 7 weeks...). We practiced saying our yes as enthusiastic as possible, just because I find that fun, but it can really look as simple as this:

Person A:Hi Grandma!
Person B:Hi Kiddo! I'm so glad you are spending the night this weekend!

🐦 CROW(E)

Defined and discussed CROW(E) which is what improvisers are trying to establish during scenes:

  • CCharacter
  • RRelationship
  • OObjective
  • WWhere
  • E...bonus: Emotion

Practiced the C of Crow with playing with walking different ways leading with different body parts and influenced by different animals

Thank You Endowment

One person stands in the middle and poses. Everyone else take turns giving one line of dialogue, trying to include as much of CROW as possible in the one line. The person making the pose says "Thank you", because it's a gift to have your offer be given meaning

Corridors

Standing in 2 lines, the front pair does a 3 line scene trying to establish CROW in those three lines.

Freeze Tag

2 improvisers start a scene and we say "freeze" when CROW has been established. The 2 improvisers in the scene freeze, then the person who said freeze taps one of them out and assumes their physical position. They then begin a new scene with a new CROW justifying the physical position that they are in.

Focus Count

Focusing on focal point the group sees how high we can count without people saying the same number. When that happens, we applaud wildly for our failure and then start back again at one.

Compliment Circle

Told the person we were watching one thing they did well in class today.
